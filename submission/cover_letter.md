I am pleased to submit the enclosed manuscript, **"Determinants of eHealth
Systems Adoption: A Systematic Review and Multi-Case Analysis,"** for
consideration as an Original Paper in *JMIR Medical Informatics*.

**What the paper contributes.** Despite sustained investment, adoption of
interoperable eHealth systems—electronic health records (EHRs) and health
information exchanges (HIEs)—remains inconsistent across organizations and
national contexts. Prior work has examined determinants in isolation. This
manuscript synthesizes the evidence into a consolidated, four-domain model
(**U-T-I-O**: User Readiness, Technical Interoperability, Institutional
Alignment, Organizational Execution) and, importantly, **operationalizes it
into a bounded 0–1 alignment index (the UAI)**—moving the model from a
descriptive taxonomy to an evaluative instrument that scores an
implementation's adoption readiness and diagnoses domain-specific gaps. The
model is grounded in a structured synthesis of 501 included studies and
illustrated through a comparative analysis of four national implementation
contexts (U.S. ONC HIE programs, NHS Shared Care Records, Estonia's national
eHealth system, and the U.S. federal EHR modernization, with the VA and DoD
programs scored separately).

**Why JMIR Medical Informatics.** The paper's methodological transparency—a
fully documented, reproducible screening pipeline with a public decision log,
an evidence-weighted scoring scheme, and candid reporting of its
limitations—aligns with the journal's emphasis on open, rigorous, and
reproducible informatics research. All underlying data and code are openly
archived with a citable DOI (see Data Availability statement).

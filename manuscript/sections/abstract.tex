\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Background:} Despite sustained investment and policy attention, adoption of interoperable eHealth systems—including electronic health records (EHRs) and health information exchanges (HIEs)—remains inconsistent across organizations and national contexts. Existing frameworks have examined isolated factors without integrating them into a unified, operationalizable model.

\textbf{Objective:} This study aims to identify and synthesize determinants of eHealth systems adoption across diverse implementation contexts and develop a consolidated model applicable to healthcare leaders and system architects.

\textbf{Methods:} An integrative evidence synthesis combining a PRISMA 2020-guided systematic review with a structured comparative multi-case analysis. Database searches of PubMed/MEDLINE and IEEE Xplore, executed in June 2026, identified 1,688 records; Scopus, Web of Science, and CINAHL could not be searched owing to unavailable institutional subscription access. After deduplication, 1,680 records were screened against a priori criteria in two documented stages, 549 reports were assessed for eligibility, and 501 studies were included for evidence extraction. The multi-case analysis examined four national implementations: U.S. ONC HIE programs, NHS Shared Care Records (UK), Estonia's national eHealth system, and the U.S. VA/DoD EHR modernization. Findings were integrated using the U-T-I-O Model.

\textbf{Results:} Adoption determinants clustered across four interdependent domains: User Readiness (usability, workflow fit, training, perceived usefulness), Technical Interoperability (system quality, semantic/syntactic interoperability, data completeness), Institutional Alignment (regulatory mandates, national digital health strategy, information governance), and Organizational Execution (leadership, change management, stakeholder engagement, resource capacity). Technical interoperability is necessary but insufficient for sustained adoption. Estonia showed the highest alignment across all four domains; the VA/DoD case demonstrated how strong institutional mandate can be undermined by gaps in user readiness and organizational execution.

\textbf{Conclusions:} The U-T-I-O Model provides an operationalizable framework for assessing eHealth adoption readiness and diagnosing domain-specific implementation gaps. Sustainable adoption requires coordinated alignment across all four domains. The model should be interpreted as an implementation-readiness and adoption-maturity framework, not as a validated predictor of patient-level clinical outcomes; establishing clinical outcome validity through the proposed U-T-I-O-OM extension is the primary direction for future research.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} eHealth adoption; electronic health records; health information exchange; interoperability; systematic review; U-T-I-O Model; implementation science

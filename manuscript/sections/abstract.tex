\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Background:} Despite sustained investment and policy attention, the adoption of interoperable eHealth systems—including electronic health records (EHRs) and health information exchanges (HIEs)—remains inconsistent across organizations and national contexts. Existing frameworks have largely examined isolated factors without integrating them into a unified, operationalizable model.

\textbf{Objective:} This study aims to identify and synthesize the determinants of eHealth systems adoption across diverse implementation contexts and to develop a consolidated adoption model applicable to healthcare leaders and system architects.

\textbf{Methods:} A PRISMA 2020-guided systematic review was conducted across three primary databases (PubMed, IEEE Xplore, Scopus) and supplementary sources, covering peer-reviewed and grey literature published between 2015 and 2026. Seventeen sources were retained as a seed set for evidence extraction and thematic synthesis (formal-search refresh pending). A parallel multi-case analysis examined four national eHealth implementations: U.S. ONC health information exchange programs, NHS Shared Care Records (UK), Estonia's national eHealth system, and the U.S. VA/DoD EHR modernization initiative. Findings were integrated using a novel analytical framework, the U-T-I-O Model.

\textbf{Results:} Adoption determinants consistently clustered across four interdependent domains: User Readiness (usability, workflow fit, training, perceived usefulness), Technical Interoperability (system quality, semantic/syntactic interoperability, data completeness), Institutional Alignment (regulatory mandates, national digital health strategy, information governance), and Organizational Execution (leadership, change management, stakeholder engagement, resource capacity). Case analysis confirmed that technical interoperability, while necessary, is insufficient for sustained adoption. Estonia represented the highest alignment across all four domains; the VA/DoD case demonstrated how strong institutional mandate can be undermined by gaps in user satisfaction and organizational execution.

\textbf{Conclusions:} The U-T-I-O Model offers an operationalizable framework for assessing eHealth adoption readiness and diagnosing implementation gaps. Sustainable adoption requires coordinated alignment across all four domains. These findings provide actionable guidance for health system leaders and establish a foundation for future research in AI-enabled diagnostic integrity and interoperable healthcare.
\end{abstract}

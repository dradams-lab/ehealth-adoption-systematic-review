\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Synthesis of Findings}
The results demonstrate that eHealth systems adoption is not determined by isolated technical capabilities, but by the alignment of user readiness, technical interoperability, institutional frameworks, and organizational execution. Across the systematic review and case analyses, these domains consistently interact to shape adoption outcomes.

\subsection{Cross-Case Comparative Insights}
The comparative analysis highlights important differences in how adoption determinants manifest across implementation contexts. Estonia represents a highly aligned system, where institutional mandate, technical infrastructure, and organizational execution converge. In contrast, the VA/DoD case illustrates how misalignment—particularly in user satisfaction and implementation governance—can undermine adoption despite strong institutional backing.

These findings suggest that institutional mandate alone is insufficient. Effective adoption requires coordinated execution across all domains, particularly at the organizational and user levels.

\subsection{Implications for Interoperability Strategy}
The findings reinforce that interoperability is not solely a technical problem. While standards and interfaces are necessary, they must be supported by governance frameworks, workflow integration, and organizational alignment.

Policies such as the HITECH Act and the 21st Century Cures Act attempt to address these challenges by realigning incentives and reducing information blocking. However, implementation effectiveness depends on how these policies translate into organizational and technical practice.

\subsection{Theoretical Perspective: Market Dynamics, Interoperability, and Ethical Alignment}

The adoption of eHealth systems occurs within a complex socio-technical and economic environment in which market dynamics play a central role. Healthcare information technology markets are characterized by vendor competition, product differentiation, and proprietary system architectures. These dynamics incentivize innovation and specialization; however, they also contribute to structural fragmentation across electronic health record (EHR) systems and health information exchanges (HIEs).

From a systems perspective, this fragmentation can be understood as an emergent property of decentralized optimization. Individual firms optimize for competitive advantage, often through proprietary data models, interfaces, and ecosystem lock-in strategies. While rational at the firm level, these behaviors can produce suboptimal outcomes at the system level, particularly in domains such as healthcare where continuity of information is essential for safe and effective care delivery.

This tension reflects a misalignment between firm-level incentives and system-level objectives. Effective healthcare delivery requires interoperability, data liquidity, and coordinated workflows across organizational boundaries. As a result, technical interoperability alone is insufficient to ensure adoption success. Instead, alignment must occur across technological, organizational, institutional, and user domains, as reflected in the proposed model:

\begin{equation}
Adoption = f(U, T, I, O)
\end{equation}

Within this framework, institutional mechanisms such as regulatory policy, interoperability standards, and governance structures play a critical role in correcting market-driven fragmentation. Policies such as the HITECH Act, the 21st Century Cures Act, and information blocking regulations represent attempts to realign incentives toward system-wide interoperability and data sharing.

Beyond policy and economics, the adoption of eHealth systems also raises broader ethical considerations. Healthcare systems inherently operate within a moral context in which patient welfare, equity, and access to information are central concerns. Interoperability, therefore, may be understood not only as a technical requirement but as an ethical imperative that supports continuity of care, reduces medical error, and promotes equitable access to health information.

This perspective suggests that sustainable eHealth adoption requires harmonization between technological innovation, market incentives, regulatory frameworks, and ethical principles. Systems that achieve this alignment are more likely to demonstrate durable adoption, scalability, and positive health outcomes across diverse populations.


\subsection{Practical Implications}
For healthcare leaders and system architects, the results suggest that adoption strategies should prioritize:

\begin{itemize}
\item Alignment between technical architecture and clinical workflows
\item Strong governance and leadership engagement
\item Investment in user training and satisfaction
\item Compliance with interoperability standards and regulatory frameworks
\end{itemize}

Organizations that address only technical interoperability without considering organizational and user factors are unlikely to achieve sustained adoption.

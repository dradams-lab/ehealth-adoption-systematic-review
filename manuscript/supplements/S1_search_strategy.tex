\section*{Supplementary File S1: Search Strategy and Review Protocol}
\label{sec:S1}

\noindent\textbf{Purpose:} Document the protocol---databases, date coverage, full
Boolean query strings, inclusion and exclusion criteria, the two-stage screening
procedure, and the U-T-I-O coding framework---so that the systematic review is
reproducible. The inclusion and exclusion criteria and the U-T-I-O coding
framework were defined a priori, prior to search execution. The complete
record-level screening log (all 1{,}680 records with decisions, reason codes, and
justifications) accompanies this file as the data file
\texttt{screening\_log\_combined.csv}.

\subsection*{1. Databases and Coverage}

\noindent\textbf{Databases searched:} PubMed/MEDLINE and IEEE Xplore. \\
\textbf{Databases planned but not searched:} Scopus, Web of Science, and CINAHL
could not be searched because institutional subscription access was unavailable
at the time of the review; their omission is an acknowledged coverage limitation
(see manuscript Limitations). A Google Scholar recall check was run as a
supplementary source. \\
\textbf{Publication window:} The empirical search applies a 2015 lower date limit
(HITECH incentive era through current FHIR mandate implementation). Foundational
theory published earlier (e.g., DeLone \& McLean, 2003) is retained as background
rather than retrieved through the empirical search. \\
\textbf{Search executed:} 4~June 2026 (PubMed/MEDLINE and IEEE Xplore).

\subsection*{3. Full Boolean Query Strings (per database)}

\noindent\textit{The PubMed/MEDLINE and IEEE Xplore query strings below were
executed on 4~June 2026. The Scopus, Web of Science, and CINAHL strings are the
pre-specified queries that were prepared but could not be executed owing to
unavailable subscription access; they are documented here for completeness and
reproducibility should access become available.
For PubMed/MEDLINE, both concept groups were restricted to the Title field to
yield a focused, single-reviewer--screenable corpus. This is a deliberate
precision-over-recall scoping decision appropriate to a single-author focused
review: it reliably captures studies whose primary subject is eHealth-systems
adoption (signalled in the title) at the cost of omitting studies that treat
adoption only peripherally in the abstract or body. This recall limitation is
acknowledged in the manuscript Limitations section.}

article type unrestricted; English-language eligibility applied at screening): \\

\noindent\textit{De-duplication was performed by exact matching on normalized DOI

\subsection*{5. Inclusion Criteria}

Records were included if they:
\begin{enumerate}
\item appeared in peer-reviewed journals or as institutional/government reports;
\item focused on eHealth, EHR, EMR, HIE, health information technology, or clinical
  interoperability;
\item addressed adoption, implementation, barriers, facilitators, or determinants of
  system uptake; and
\item employed empirical, systematic review, scoping review, or framework-based
  research designs.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{6. Exclusion Criteria}

Records were excluded if they:
\begin{enumerate}
\item were opinion editorials, letters, or conference abstracts without full
  methodology;
\item addressed non-healthcare IT systems without healthcare application;
\item were published in languages other than English; or
\item involved the primary collection of identifiable human-subject data.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{7. Screening and Data-Extraction Procedure}

Retrieved records were deduplicated as described in Section~4, leaving 1{,}680
unique records. Title and abstract screening was applied to all 1{,}680 records
against the criteria above in two documented stages:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Stage 1 (sensitive screen):} records were classified against the
  base inclusion/exclusion criteria; 433 were excluded as off-topic with respect
  to eHealth-systems adoption, outside the 2015 window, or non-research
  publication types.
\item \textbf{Stage 2 (eligibility refinement):} the 1{,}247 stage-1 survivors
  were re-screened against the review's precise scope---adoption of interoperable
  eHealth systems (EHR/EMR/HIE/health-IT infrastructure) at the organizational or
  national/system level---excluding a further 698 records that were
  patient/consumer-facing acceptance studies, single-condition digital-health
  interventions, or studies using an EHR only as a clinical-data source.
\end{itemize}

\noindent In total, 1{,}131 records were excluded at title/abstract and 549 were
sought for full-text eligibility assessment. Full text was retrieved from PubMed
Central for 272 openly available records; the remaining 277 were assessed on their
abstracts, with the evidence basis recorded per study. Full-text assessment
excluded a further 48 reports (reasons itemised in Supplementary File~S2),
yielding 501 included studies.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Screening procedure and reproducibility.} Screening and
extraction were performed with the assistance of a large language model applying
the fixed a priori rubric to each record's title, abstract, and metadata (and,
where retrieved, full text). Every decision was recorded together with a reason
code and a one-line justification. The complete decision log for all 1{,}680
records is provided as \texttt{screening\_log\_combined.csv}, and the extraction
for all 501 included studies as \texttt{included\_studies\_evidence.csv}, so that
each stage of the funnel is reproducible from the logged data. This
machine-assisted procedure, and the fact that 277 eligibility decisions rested on
abstracts rather than retrieved full text, are noted as limitations in the
manuscript. Data extraction applied the U-T-I-O coding framework (construct
definitions and example codes in the manuscript Coding Schema table; case-level
scoring rubric in Supplementary File~S3).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Protocol registration:} Prospective protocol registration was not
undertaken: the review's outcomes are implementation and adoption determinants
rather than patient health outcomes, placing it outside PROSPERO's registration
scope, and the synthesis draws exclusively on published literature and public
documentation. The protocol documented here was defined a priori, prior to
search execution.
